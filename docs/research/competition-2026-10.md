# Competitive analysis — Mastermind Council (council.dravec.org)

Date: 2026-10-27 · Prices seen on 2026-10-27 unless a row says otherwise · Track: commercial · Read with `MOAT.md` and `flywheel/docs/standards/moat.md`.

## Summary

Twelve products overlap with Mastermind Council in four groups: AI advisory boards (SynthBoard, Moot, Kin), historical-figure chat (Hello History, Character.AI, Historical Figures, TalkTo AI, Agora Cosmica), Stoic apps (Stoic., Stoa) and AI journals (Mindsera, Rosebud). Paid tiers run from $3.99/month (Hello History) to $99/month (SynthBoard Ultra, monthly billing); the consumer journal cluster sits at $8.99–$14.99/month. The closest competitor is **SynthBoard**. It already ships most of our structure: advisers who argue at one table, a decision memo that records dissent and a dated first step, a follow-up on that date, memory with per-fact forgetting, and an MCP server on every plan, from $16.67/month on annual billing. Its experts are role archetypes (CFO, Skeptic) grounded in live web research, not historical thinkers quoted from their own works. No product we found checks each quote an adviser uses against the thinker's actual text. Agora Cosmica comes nearest, with fact-check sheets per figure, but its founder says live answers can still hallucinate and that grounding "has not been measured". Historical-figure chat apps are criticised in the press and on HN for inventing facts and for every figure sounding the same. Stoic apps are journals and meditation libraries with quotes and no deliberation. Our distinct offer is classical thinkers held to their own words, plus a decision record the user owns. The moat is the record, not the personas. Entrant difficulty is **1 today** (designed for 2, not yet earned) and the **target is 3**.

## Competitors

### 1. SynthBoard — AI board of 24 experts
- **URL:** https://synthboard.ai (pricing: https://synthboard.ai/pricing)
- **Price/model (2026-10-27):** Freemium. Free $0 (150 credits/month + 250 at signup, up to 3 experts per table, standard models). Pro $16.67/month billed $200/year. Max $41.67/month billed $500/year. Ultra $82.50/month billed $990/year. The FAQ on the same page gives monthly-billing prices of $20 / $50 / $99. Capterra lists the starting price as US$16.67.
- **Audience:** founders, operators and small-business owners making strategic decisions.
- **Does well:** a board that "argues at one table". Each board sits on different models so the views differ. The memo states the call with a confidence level, who disagreed, what would flip the call, and a dated first step. The chief of staff follows up on that date. Memory shows the source of each fact, any fact can be forgotten, and "Start fresh" wipes everything with 24 h to undo. Export to PDF and Markdown, read-only share links. MCP server and REST API on every plan. Integrations with Notion, Google Docs, Slack and Gmail.
- **Does badly:** experts are generic roles, not people with a body of work, so nothing is cited from a primary text. Grounding is web research. Credit-metered pricing is hard to predict. No voice on the pricing page. We found no app-store listing, Reddit thread or HN thread with user reviews, so there is no independent quality signal.

### 2. Moot — Debate Anything (Studio 42)
- **URL:** https://apps.apple.com/app/id6756263281
- **Price/model (2026-10-27):** free download, but Moot Pro is required to start a debate. Listed in-app purchases: Moot Pro $14.99/month, $39.99/year, plus $29.99, $49.99, $69.99 and $79.99 (periods not stated). Moot Max $24.99 and $29.99. Council Seat Expansion $4.99. "The Stoic Advisor" $2.99.
- **Audience:** consumers with personal and lifestyle decisions (category: Lifestyle, 16+).
- **Does well:** five fixed archetypes (General, Sage, Skeptic, Diplomat, Architect) argue and each casts a final vote. Custom councils, mid-debate interjections, a pre-debate "Inquisitor", and reflection on past debates.
- **Does badly:** rated 4.3/5 from 248 ratings. Reviews report invented facts (it attributed feelings to a friend that the user never described), advisers that "share a single underlying voice", votes against the position the adviser argued, an Inquisitor that repeats what the user already said, a self-harm safety concern, and a price that is too high for what it delivers. The developer has replied that it is working on personality, voting consistency and safeguards. No voice. A Stoic adviser is sold as a $2.99 add-on with no grounding in Stoic texts.

### 3. Kin — Personal AI Advisors
- **URL:** https://mykin.ai · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/kin-personal-ai-advisors/id6473448146
- **Price/model (2026-10-27, US App Store):** free download with in-app purchases. Standard $21.99/month, $44.99/quarter, $169.99/year. Premium $20.00/month, $39.00/quarter, $189.00/year. Pro $43.99/month, $89.99/quarter, $339.99/year. (These are the store figures as listed, including Premium monthly being cheaper than Standard.)
- **Audience:** individuals who want life coaching across work, relationships, values, energy and social confidence.
- **Does well:** an "Advisory Board" of five domain advisors, a persistent memory graph you can edit, voice calls, a spoken morning briefing ("Daily Edge"), and structured Sessions. The listing says memory stays on-device.
- **Does badly:** rated 4.5/5 from 187 ratings. A November 2025 review complains that features moved behind a paywall ("no more journal entries, no more fun custom AIs") and that the subscription is "higher than most others". Another review says a feedback prompt interrupted a chat. The privacy label lists health, location and contact data linked to identity, which conflicts with the "private by design" message. Advisers are life domains, not thinkers.

### 4. Hello History
- **URL:** https://apps.apple.com/us/app/hello-history-ai-chat/id1659654111
- **Price/model (2026-10-27, US App Store):** free with in-app purchases. Weekly $1.99 and $3.99, Monthly $3.99 and $5.99, Yearly $34.99. Subscribers get up to 10,000 messages a month. A 2023 teacher newsletter reported $5.49/week, $7.99/month and $47.99/year. Prices have changed over time.
- **Audience:** students and teachers (one directory says upper elementary through high school), plus curious adults.
- **Does well:** 400+ figures, curated topics, voice since version 3.0, a simple UI. Rated 4.6/5 from 938 ratings.
- **Does badly:** one figure at a time, entertainment and education framing, no decision output. The listing itself tells users to check the facts the AI generates. A directory review notes it is "not always 100% historically accurate". No negative reviews were visible on the store page.

### 5. Character.AI
- **URL:** https://character.ai
- **Price/model:** free tier with ads and gated swipes. c.ai+ $9.99/month or $94.99/year (RecurDash checked this on character.ai on 2026-08-07; one source says the official pricing page 404s and the price appears only in the in-app prompt).
- **Audience:** mass-market entertainment and companionship, skewing young.
- **Does well:** a huge catalogue that includes Socrates and Marcus Aurelius bots, unlimited voice calls on c.ai+, "better memory" on c.ai+.
- **Does badly:** the product's own disclaimer reads "Remember, everything characters say is made up!" User-made characters have no grounding and no decision structure. The brand is about companionship and role-play, which works against "serious decision".

### 6. Historical Figures (Sidhant Chadda)
- **URL:** App Store listing not found in this pass. Coverage: Rolling Stone (link below), Black Enterprise.
- **Price/model:** not found.
- **Audience:** general consumers. About 20,000 figures, built on GPT-3. About 9,000 downloads shortly after its January 2023 launch.
- **Does well:** breadth. The figure for a niche question is usually there.
- **Does badly:** in Rolling Stone's test, the app got basic biography wrong (it gave J. Edgar Hoover a wrong account of his mother's death) and contradicted itself when challenged. The app later added a disclaimer asking users to verify facts. I could not fetch the full Rolling Stone article (paywalled). These details come from the search excerpt and a Substack write-up.

### 7. TalkTo AI
- **URL:** App Store "TalkTo AI: Chat AI Characters". HN launch thread: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=34847842
- **Price/model:** $20/month according to a commenter in the 2023-02-18 HN thread. No current price found.
- **Audience:** consumers and education.
- **Does well:** a low-friction way to talk to Socrates or Marie Curie.
- **Does badly:** HN commenters reported that Jane Austen gave a wrong year, that Socrates and Newton "spoke basically the same", that many questions got no response, and that the app is a thin prompt layer over ChatGPT. The developer said he could not guarantee that "everything the bot says is necessarily true or correct".

### 8. Agora Cosmica
- **URL:** https://agoracosmica.org · source: github.com/chipmates/agoracosmica · HN: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48459913
- **Price/model (2026-10-27):** free and nonprofit (AGPL-3.0). 30 free messages a day with no signup, bring your own OpenRouter key for more, or self-host in Local Mode.
- **Audience:** learners and the philosophy-curious. Privacy-minded, EU-hosted.
- **Does well:** this is the closest product to our grounding claim. Thirty figures, among them Marcus Aurelius, Buddha and C.G. Jung. Each figure ships a fact-check sheet listing what is verified and what is recreated, plus a "shadow" section on the figure's flaws. Voice runs on its own German GPU server. Conversations live only in the browser, with no tracking.
- **Does badly:** the founder says on HN that live conversation can still hallucinate, and that grounding "helps, but to be honest we have not measured it". Quotes are not checked one by one. No Seneca, Socrates or Confucius. No decision record, commitments or account memory. HN commenters called the concept "disrespectful" and "icky", and objected to "placing words in their mouth". The same ethics objection will reach us.

### 9. Stoic. (journal & mental health)
- **URL:** https://apps.apple.com/app/id1312926037 · help: https://help.getstoic.com
- **Price/model (2026-10-27, US App Store):** free journaling, plus Premium at $6.99, $17.99, $26.99 and $39.99 (a third-party review maps these to $6.99/month and $39.99/year). AI plans $12.99, $69.99 and $99.99 (Premium + AI is $99.99/year per the same review). Lifetime $299.00. Trials: 7 days for Premium, 3 days for Premium + AI, annual plan only.
- **Audience:** mass-market iOS journaling and mental-health users. Editors' Choice.
- **Does well:** rated 4.8/5 from about 36K ratings. Morning and evening reflection, AI mentors by *style* ("calm and philosophical" to "practical"), pattern analysis of past entries, smart notifications. Private on-device options.
- **Does badly:** "Stoic" is branding. Mentors are styles, not Seneca or Marcus, and the AI runs on OpenAI with no quote grounding. Complaints collected on unitQ say the lifetime plan disappeared after AI launched, and one user was charged after cancelling a free trial. Apple only, no web app (per a competitor's blog, so treat that with care). No deliberation and no decision record.

### 10. Stoa (Stoic meditation)
- **URL:** https://www.stoameditation.com · https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daily-stoic-philosophy-stoa/id1446345567 · HN launch: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=21692870
- **Price/model (2026-10-27):** the official site says "check the Stoa app for the most recent pricing". The US App Store lists Weekly $3.99, Monthly $9.99 / $11.99 / $13.99, Yearly $39.99 / $49.99 / $59.99 / $79.99, without mapping prices to plans. The Google Play listing (AppFollow) shows $9.99/month and $89.99/year. 7-day trial.
- **Audience:** serious Stoicism practitioners. The team holds philosophy degrees (Caleb Ontiveros MA, Michael Tremblay PhD).
- **Does well:** rated 4.8/5 from 998 ratings. 100+ hours of guided audio, thousands of real Stoic quotes and texts, expert conversations (Pigliucci, Robertson). This is the most credible classical brand in the group.
- **Does badly:** no AI conversation, no deliberation, no decision output. The free tier is limited to the journal, quotes and introductory meditations. Too many unmapped price points on the store page. No negative reviews were visible.

### 11. Mindsera — AI journal with "minds" and mental models
- **URL:** https://www.mindsera.com
- **Price/model (2026-10-27):** Curious free tier. Genius $14.99/month, or $129/year ($10.75/month). A $99/year promotion appears at times (per Life Note's review; Life Note is a competitor).
- **Audience:** reflective professionals who like frameworks (Naval, Munger and Tim Urban are quoted on the site as users of frameworks).
- **Does well:** 50+ thinking frameworks, "Minds" that annotate your writing (directories name Marcus Aurelius, Jung and Socrates among the mentors; the site no longer names them), voice and Call Mode, "Ask your journal" over all entries, encryption, no training on user data, export. Rated 4.8/5 from 246 reviews (2026, per Life Note).
- **Does badly:** mentor personas are not grounded in quotes. Cons per Life Note's review: a steep learning curve, a thin free tier, features optimised for English only, no iPad app, and copy/paste friction on mobile. It is a single-writer journal, not advisers debating. No commitments with reminders.

### 12. Rosebud — AI journal
- **URL:** https://www.rosebud.app · notice: https://help.rosebud.app/account/changes-to-the-free-plan
- **Price/model (2026-10-27):** $12.99/month, or $107.99/year ($8.99/month). The free plan was retired on 2026-09-30 because the new memory system "requires significantly more resources to run". Student and disability discounts are available.
- **Audience:** a mainstream therapy-adjacent journaling audience. Shows a "HIPAA aligned" badge.
- **Does well:** long-term memory, voice journaling, goal work, IFS-guided journaling, customisable personas. Rated 4.9/5 from 3.4K ratings (Life Note, 2026-10-22). Export of all data.
- **Does badly:** removing the free tier is a direct signal that memory is expensive to serve. That cost applies to us too. A Macaron review calls it "one of the most expensive dedicated journaling apps" and notes that the Terms let anonymised content be used for training. No historical thinkers and no deliberation.

**Substitutes, not counted above:** a "board of advisors" prompt in ChatGPT or Claude (PCWorld, 2026). MindStudio's free "Roast Room" Claude skill, in which business-figure personas debate for three rounds. The pitch "Board" on Indie Hackers ("tired of asking ChatGPT if my ideas were good. It always said yes"); its price was not found and the page returned 403. These set the floor: a debating council of personas costs a user $0 and one prompt.

## Where we win

**The moat bet.** Two things together: a decision journal the user owns (decision, reasoning, assumptions, next action, review trigger, plus commitments with outcomes) that the council reads back as "Last time…", and a classical brand that rests on one claim no competitor makes: *every quote is checked against the thinker's own text, with the passage linked*. SynthBoard has the structure but no thinkers. Agora Cosmica and Stoa have the classical credibility but no decision loop. Character-style apps have neither and are publicly known for inventing facts.

**What we do not rely on** (per moat.md): the persona prompts, the roster or the facilitator script. Assume a competitor has them. We also do not rely on the quote corpus by itself. Seneca, Marcus Aurelius, Confucius and the others are public domain, so a competent dev can build retrieval plus a checker in weeks. The corpus earns its place as the proof behind the brand claim and as the private eval set, not as a secret.

**Entrant difficulty — before (today, 2026-10-27): 1.**
Test: "a competent solo dev with our prompt, two weeks, $0." With our prompts and code they ship a lookalike: debate, facilitator, journal, reminders, MCP. SynthBoard shows the structure is already a shipped commodity. `MOAT.md` scores the design at 2 ("needs months of our accumulated data"), but the score counts only data that exists. The app is in password-gated private beta, so per-user history is a few weeks deep for a handful of users, and there is no audience for the daily question yet. Today the entrant would ship it and have no users, which is a 1. That is below the 2–3 target band and at the stage-4 kill line if it stays there.

**Target after (stage 6–7): 3.**
- **Reaching 2 — accumulated per-user data.** Each signed-in user adds journal entries, commitments and outcomes (done, later, drop) every week. The "Last time…" opener and review triggers make that history useful, so leaving costs something. Evidence the gate needs: the stage-7 cohort split (users with 8 or more weeks of entries retain above the 14 % DAU/MAU gen-AI baseline), and at least one user asking for export or continuity. This needs the free signed-in tier to hold memory. Rosebud dropped its free plan because memory was expensive to serve (2026-09-30), so cap memory depth per tier rather than give it up.
- **Reaching 3 — a brand we built.** "Verified-quote council" becomes a named, public method: a quote page per adviser that links each passage to its source edition, a published check rate from the ≥ 20-case eval set in CI, and the daily question's share card (the split, never the user's text) seeded into communities that already police misattribution (Modern Stoicism has written on fake Marcus Aurelius quotes). An entrant would then lack the audience and the reputation for not making things up, not the code. MCP in Claude and ChatGPT is a distribution channel that supports this. It is not a moat, because SynthBoard already has one.
- **Why not 4:** there is no org graph or compliance artifact. A team tier could add one later, but that is out of scope for this track.

**Exposure.** SynthBoard could add "inspired by" historical personas in a sprint. Our answer is the verification claim and the classical roster. Our price has to sit under its $16.67–$20/month for the same job. Consumer journals cluster at $8.99–$14.99/month, and Stoic users already pay $39.99–$99.99/year.
