What the classics wrote about death

For the Stoics, remembering that time ends is a way of deciding well now, not a morbid exercise.

Every line below appears word for word in the public-domain translation named under it; a script checked each one against the text of that edition. The link opens the letter, book or edition it came from.

Marcus Aurelius

“Do not act as if thou wert going to live ten thousand years. Death hangs over thee. While thou livest, while it is in thy power, be good.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.17 · trans. George Long · source
“Since it is possible that thou mayest depart from life this very moment, regulate every act and thought accordingly.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, II.11 · trans. George Long · source
“How quickly all things disappear,—in the universe the bodies themselves, but in time the remembrance of them.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, II.12 · trans. George Long · source
“We must make haste, then, not only because we are daily nearer to death, but also because the conception of things and the understanding of them cease first.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, III.1 · trans. George Long · source
“To conclude, always observe how ephemeral and worthless human things are, and what was yesterday a little mucus, to-morrow will be a mummy or ashes.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.48 · trans. George Long · source
“there in my opinion he ought to stay and to abide the hazard, taking nothing into the reckoning, either death or anything else, before the baseness [of deserting his post].”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, VII.45 · trans. George Long · source
“Magnanimity is the elevation of the intelligent part above the pleasurable or painful sensations of the flesh, and above that poor thing called fame, and death, and all such things.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, X.8 · trans. George Long · source
“those who have been most conspicuous by the greatest fame or misfortunes or enmities or fortunes of any kind: then think where are they all now? Smoke and ash and a tale, or not even a tale.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, XII.27 · trans. George Long · source
“He who has a vehement desire for posthumous fame does not consider that every one of those who remember him will himself also die very soon;”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.19 · trans. George Long · source
“Short is the little which remains to thee of life. Live as on a mountain.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, X.15 · trans. George Long · source

Seneca

“No evil is great which is the last evil of all.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 4 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Therefore I declare to you: he is lord of your life that scorns his own.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 4 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Believe me, those who seem to be busied with nothing are busied with the greater tasks; they are dealing at the same time with things mortal and things immortal.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 8 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Let us cherish and love old age; for it is full of pleasure if one knows how to use it.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 12 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“We have spent our lives on the high seas; let us die in harbour.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 19 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Men do not care how nobly they live, but only how long, although it is within the reach of every man to live nobly, but within no man’s power to live long.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 22 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“We must make it our aim already to have lived long enough.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 23 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“There is no fixed count of our years. You do not know where death awaits you; so be ready for it everywhere.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 26 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“There is only one chain which binds us to life, and that is the love of life.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 26 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Of this one thing make sure against your dying day,—let your faults die before you die.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 27 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source

Socrates

“For the fear of death is indeed the pretence of wisdom, and not real wisdom, being a pretence of knowing the unknown; and no one knows whether death, which men in their fear apprehend to be the greatest evil, may not be the greatest good.”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Apology · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“Wherefore, O judges, be of good cheer about death, and know of a certainty, that no evil can happen to a good man, either in life or after death.”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Apology · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“The hour of departure has arrived, and we go our ways—I to die, and you to live. Which is better God only knows.”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Apology · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“Leave me then, Crito, to fulfil the will of God, and to follow whither he leads.”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Crito · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“Can any man be courageous who has the fear of death in him?”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Republic III · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“Reflect: our principle is that the good man will not consider death terrible to any other good man who is his comrade.”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Republic III · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source

The Buddha

“Earnestness is the path of immortality (Nirvana), thoughtlessness the path of death.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, Dhammapada v. 21 · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“`All created things perish,' he who knows and sees this becomes passive in pain; this is the way to purity.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, Dhammapada v. 277 · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“Conditions fall apart. Persist with diligence.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, DN 16:6.7.3 (Sujato) · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“Please, Ānanda, you must all strive and practice for your heart's goal! Meditate diligent, keen, and resolute for your heart's goal!”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, DN 16:5.10.3 (Sujato) · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source

Confucius

“The determined scholar and the man of virtue will not seek to live at the expense of injuring their virtue. They will even sacrifice their lives to preserve their virtue complete.”Confucius, Analects, XV.8 · trans. James Legge · source
“Virtue is more to man than either water or fire. I have seen men die from treading on water and fire, but I have never seen a man die from treading the course of virtue.”Confucius, Analects, XV.34 · trans. James Legge · source

Benjamin Franklin

“There will be sleeping enough in the Grave”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, The Way to Wealth · source
“My distemper was a pleurisy, which very nearly carried me off”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, Autobiography, ch. VII (Beginning Business in Philadelphia) · source

Maria Montessori

“just as the lungs forced into immobility, would die instantly and the whole organism with them.”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. XXI "General Review of Discipline" · trans. Anne E. George · source
“Truly our social life is too often only the darkening and the death of the natural life that is in us.”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. XXII "Conclusions and Impressions" · trans. Anne E. George · source
