What the classics wrote about time

How long a life is depends less on its length than on how the hours are used. These are the lines to read when a decision keeps being put off.

Every line below appears word for word in the public-domain translation named under it; a script checked each one against the text of that edition. The link opens the letter, book or edition it came from.

Marcus Aurelius

“Do not act as if thou wert going to live ten thousand years. Death hangs over thee. While thou livest, while it is in thy power, be good.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.17 · trans. George Long · source
“I am rising to the work of a human being. Why then am I dissatisfied if I am going to do the things for which I exist and for which I was brought into the world?”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, V.1 · trans. George Long · source
“Since it is possible that thou mayest depart from life this very moment, regulate every act and thought accordingly.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, II.11 · trans. George Long · source
“How quickly all things disappear,—in the universe the bodies themselves, but in time the remembrance of them.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, II.12 · trans. George Long · source
“We must make haste, then, not only because we are daily nearer to death, but also because the conception of things and the understanding of them cease first.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, III.1 · trans. George Long · source
“Throwing away then all things, hold to these only which are few; and besides, bear in mind that every man lives only this present time, which is an indivisible point, and that all the rest of his life is either past or it is uncertain.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, III.10 · trans. George Long · source
“Hasten then to the end which thou hast before thee, and, throwing away idle hopes, come to thy own aid, if thou carest at all for thyself, while it is in thy power.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, III.14 · trans. George Long · source
“In a word, thy life is short. Thou must turn to profit the present by the aid of reason and justice.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.26 · trans. George Long · source
“Time is like a river made up of the events which happen, and a violent stream; for as soon as a thing has been seen, it is carried away, and another comes in its place, and this will be carried away too.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.43 · trans. George Long · source
“To conclude, always observe how ephemeral and worthless human things are, and what was yesterday a little mucus, to-morrow will be a mummy or ashes.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, IV.48 · trans. George Long · source
“Short is the little which remains to thee of life. Live as on a mountain.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, X.15 · trans. George Long · source

Seneca

“Nothing, Lucilius, is ours, except time.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 1 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“While we are postponing, life speeds by.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 1 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Everywhere means nowhere. When a person spends all his time in foreign travel, he ends by having many acquaintances, but no friends.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 2 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“The present alone can make no man wretched.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 5 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“That man is happiest, and is secure in his own possession of himself, who can await the morrow without apprehension.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 12 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Now there are short and simple exercises which tire the body rapidly, and so save our time; and time is something of which we ought to keep strict account.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 15 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“We must make it our aim already to have lived long enough.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 23 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“But indeed to one who is active no day is long. So let us lengthen our lives; for the duty and the proof of life consist in action.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 122 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source

Benjamin Franklin

“It would be thought a hard Government that should tax its People one-tenth Part of their Time, to be employed in its Service.”Benjamin Franklin, The Way to Wealth · source
“But dost thou love Life, then do not squander Time, for that's the stuff Life is made of”Benjamin Franklin, The Way to Wealth · source
“There will be sleeping enough in the Grave”Benjamin Franklin, The Way to Wealth · source
“Lost Time is never found again; and what we call Time enough, always proves little enough”Benjamin Franklin, The Way to Wealth · source
“Have you somewhat to do To-morrow, do it To-day”Benjamin Franklin, The Way to Wealth · source
“A Life of Leisure and a Life of Laziness are two things”Benjamin Franklin, The Way to Wealth · source
“Lose no time; be always employ'd in something useful; cut off all unnecessary actions”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography, ch. IX (Plan for Attaining Moral Perfection, the thirteen virtues) · source

Confucius

“It is not easy to find a man who has learned for three years without coming to be good.”Confucius, Analects, VIII.12 · trans. James Legge · source
“If a truly royal ruler were to arise, it would still require a generation, and then virtue would prevail.”Confucius, Analects, XIII.12 · trans. James Legge · source
“Do not be desirous to have things done quickly; do not look at small advantages. Desire to have things done quickly prevents their being done thoroughly.”Confucius, Analects, XIII.17 · trans. James Legge · source
“If a man take no thought about what is distant, he will find sorrow near at hand.”Confucius, Analects, XV.11 · trans. James Legge · source

Socrates

“The hour of departure has arrived, and we go our ways—I to die, and you to live. Which is better God only knows.”Socrates, Plato’s Apology · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“In the leisure spoken of by you, which a freeman can always command: he has his talk out in peace, and, like ourselves, he wanders at will from one subject to another, and from a second to a third”Socrates, Plato’s Theaetetus · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“You know also that the beginning is the most important part of any work, especially in the case of a young and tender thing; for that is the time at which the character is being formed and the desired impression is more readily taken.”Socrates, Plato’s Republic II · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source

Maria Montessori

“Who does not know that to _teach_ a child to feed himself, to wash and dress himself, is a much more tedious and difficult work, calling for infinitely greater patience, than feeding, washing and dressing the child one's self?”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. V "Discipline" · trans. Anne E. George · source
“The children are initiated into the virtue of _patience and into confident expectation_, which is a form of faith and of philosophy of life.”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. X "Nature in Education" · trans. Anne E. George · source
