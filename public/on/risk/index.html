What the classics wrote about risk

Acting without certainty: what can be lost, what is lost by never moving, and how to tell the two apart.

Every line below appears word for word in the public-domain translation named under it; a script checked each one against the text of that edition. The link opens the letter, book or edition it came from.

The Buddha

“If a traveller does not meet with one who is his better, or his equal, let him firmly keep to his solitary journey; there is no companionship with a fool.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, Dhammapada v. 61 · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“If by leaving a small pleasure one sees a great pleasure, let a wise man leave the small pleasure, and look to the great.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, Dhammapada v. 290 · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“The thirst of a thoughtless man grows like a creeper; he runs from life to life, like a monkey seeking fruit in the forest.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, Dhammapada v. 334 · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“Mendicants, I will teach you a simile of the teaching as a raft: for crossing over, not for holding on.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, MN 22:13.1 (Sujato) · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“Furthermore, with his legitimate wealth he protects himself against losses from such things as fire, water, kings, bandits, or unloved heirs. He keeps himself safe.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, AN 5.41:3.1–3.3 (Sujato) · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source
“One portion is to enjoy. Two parts invest in work. And the fourth should be kept for times of trouble.”The Buddha, Dhammapada and the suttas, DN 31:26.19–22 (Sujato) · trans. F. Max Müller; Bhikkhu Sujato · source

Benjamin Franklin

“Diligence is the Mother of Good-luck”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, The Way to Wealth · source
“for want of a Nail the Shoe was lost; for want of a Shoe the Horse was lost; and for want of a Horse the Rider was lost, being overtaken and slain by the Enemy; all for the want of Care about a Horse-shoe Nail”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, The Way to Wealth · source
“Beware of little expenses; A small Leak will sink a great Ship”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, The Way to Wealth · source
“If you would know the Value of Money, go and try to borrow some; for, he that goes a borrowing goes a sorrowing”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, The Way to Wealth · source
“he had too much self-confidence, too high an opinion of the validity of regular troops, and too mean a one of both Americans and Indians”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, Autobiography, ch. XVI (Braddock's Expedition) · source
“I had conceiv'd some doubts and some fears for the event of the campaign”Benjamin Franklin, Autobiography and The Way to Wealth, Autobiography, ch. XVI (Braddock's Expedition) · source

Confucius

“My doctrines make no way. I will get upon a raft, and float about on the sea.”Confucius, Analects, V.6 · trans. James Legge · source
“If the search for riches is sure to be successful, though I should become a groom with whip in hand to get them, I will do so.”Confucius, Analects, VII.11 · trans. James Legge · source
“The man, who in the view of gain, thinks of righteousness; who in the view of danger is prepared to give up his life; and who does not forget an old agreement however far back it extends”Confucius, Analects, XIV.13 · trans. James Legge · source
“The determined scholar and the man of virtue will not seek to live at the expense of injuring their virtue. They will even sacrifice their lives to preserve their virtue complete.”Confucius, Analects, XV.8 · trans. James Legge · source
“Virtue is more to man than either water or fire. I have seen men die from treading on water and fire, but I have never seen a man die from treading the course of virtue.”Confucius, Analects, XV.34 · trans. James Legge · source

Maria Montessori

“progress comes from the _new things that are born_, and these, not being foreseen, are not rewarded with prizes: rather, they often carry the leader to martyrdom.”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. I "A Critical Consideration of the New Pedagogy in its Relation to Modern Science" · trans. Anne E. George · source
“The grand gentleman who has too many servants not only grows constantly more and more dependent upon them, until he is, finally, actually their slave, but his muscles grow weak through inactivity and finally lose their natural capacity for action.”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. V "Discipline" · trans. Anne E. George · source
“An armed brigand stops the carriage with the well-known phrase, "Your money or your life."”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. V "Discipline" · trans. Anne E. George · source
“just as the lungs forced into immobility, would die instantly and the whole organism with them.”Maria Montessori, The Montessori Method, ch. XXI "General Review of Discipline" · trans. Anne E. George · source

Seneca

“It is equally faulty to trust everyone and to trust no one.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 3 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“If you would not have a man flinch when the crisis comes, train him before it comes.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 18 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“Establish business relations with poverty.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 18 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source
“When a man does not know what harbour he is making for, no wind is the right wind.”Seneca, Letters to Lucilius, Letter 71 · trans. Richard M. Gummere · source

Socrates

“For the fear of death is indeed the pretence of wisdom, and not real wisdom, being a pretence of knowing the unknown; and no one knows whether death, which men in their fear apprehend to be the greatest evil, may not be the greatest good.”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Apology · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“But that we shall be better and braver and less helpless if we think that we ought to enquire, than we should have been if we indulged in the idle fancy that there was no knowing and no use in seeking to know”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Meno · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“Can any man be courageous who has the fear of death in him?”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Republic III · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source
“Until philosophers are kings, or the kings and princes of this world have the spirit and power of philosophy, and political greatness and wisdom meet in one”Socrates, Plato’s dialogues, Republic V · trans. Benjamin Jowett · source

Marcus Aurelius

“there in my opinion he ought to stay and to abide the hazard, taking nothing into the reckoning, either death or anything else, before the baseness [of deserting his post].”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, VII.45 · trans. George Long · source
“For it is best to reach this object, and if thou dost fail, let thy failure be in attempting this.”Marcus Aurelius, Meditations, X.12 · trans. George Long · source
